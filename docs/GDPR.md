# Erasure requests

Under UK GDPR Article 17 a person can ask you to delete what you hold about
them. You have **one month** to do it and to tell them you have. This is the
screen that does it: **Admin → Erasure requests** (owners only).

Search by whatever they gave you — an email address, a phone number, or their
name. It looks across enquiries, bookings and gift vouchers together, because
someone who booked with one address and enquired with another is still one
person, and answering half the request is not answering it.

---

## Two decisions built into this, and why

### It anonymises; it does not delete

The rows stay. The person comes out of them.

A booking is a trading record — covers on a night, a deposit taken — and
deleting it falsifies the accounts, which HMRC requires kept for six years.
Article 17(3)(b) and (e) exist for exactly this: the right to erasure does not
override a legal obligation to retain, and it does not override the
establishment or defence of legal claims. So the personal data goes and the
transaction stays.

What is removed: name, email, phone, company, occasion, free-text notes, the
internal note staff wrote, marketing consent, and the self-service link from
their confirmation email. **And any allergy or dietary note** — that is health
data, special category under Article 9, and the most sensitive thing a
restaurant holds about anybody.

### A live gift voucher blocks it

If the person holds a voucher with a balance, erasure is refused and the codes
are named.

That balance is money the restaurant owes to whoever presents the code.
Stripping the name off it does not cancel the debt — it makes the debt
unattributable. The restaurant still owes it and can no longer tell to whom.
That is worse for the customer, not better.

Settle it or cancel it on the Gift vouchers screen first, then come back.
Cancelling is a real financial decision, which is why it is owner-only and why
this screen will not do it for you.

---

## What this cannot reach

Said here and on the screen itself, because a promise that quietly
under-delivers is what turns a complaint into a fine.

**Backups taken before today** still hold the data as it was. They are pruned
on a rolling basis — thirty by default — so it ages out on its own. ICO
guidance accepts that, provided the data is not restored back into live use.
If you ever do restore a backup, **run the erasure again**.

**Emails already sent** are outside this system: their confirmation in their
own inbox, and the copy in the restaurant's.

---

## Before you erase

- **Check it is really them.** An erasure request sent from someone else's
  address is a way to delete a rival's booking, or to cover a trail.
- You may refuse a request that is manifestly unfounded or excessive, and you
  may charge for repeats — but you have to say so, and say why.
- **Reply to them either way**, within one month. "We hold nothing for you" is
  a complete and correct answer when the search finds nothing.

## The record

Every erasure is written to the Activity log and emailed to the owner
immediately. The entry records **a fingerprint** of what was searched for, not
the address itself — the proof that a request was answered must not become the
place the erased data survives.
